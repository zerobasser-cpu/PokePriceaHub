How this forecast is calculated

This tool reads card names and counts only — it has no knowledge of attack damage, HP, abilities, or how specific cards interact. It scores three things it can actually infer from a list: how much of the deck is dedicated to drawing and searching for cards (consistency), how much of the Pokémon lineup is made up of multi-prize attackers like ex, V, VMAX, VSTAR, or GX cards (offensive power, which is also a prize-giveaway risk), and how concentrated the attacker lineup is around a small core versus spread across many one-ofs (focus).

Those three scores are weighted and compared to produce a percentage split. It is not a game simulation, and it can't account for type matchups, specific tech counters, or the skill of either player.

The suggested additions under each deck come from the same rule-based checks — flagging things like a thin Supporter line, no bench-hitting Trainer card, or no way to recover from a retreat cost — compared against what's already in the list. They're generic structural gaps, not picks tailored to your specific matchup or format.

Suggested cards are chosen to reflect the Standard format following the April 2026 rotation, to the best of publicly available information as of this tool's last update. Exact tournament legality depends on a card's specific printing (its regulation mark), so double-check any suggestion against the official Pok\u00e9mon TCG card database before adding it to a tournament deck.

This is a heuristic estimate, not a match simulation. Real outcomes depend on draw order, sequencing, prize trades, and player decisions — treat this as deck-building feedback, not a prediction of what will actually happen on the table.
